01Meet Dany

Hi, I'm Dany! I'm Italian, and I recently travelled to Brazil with a friend. We joined an organised tour with a travel agency, and the group was brilliantly organised. From the beaches of Rio to the white dunes of the north, it turned out to be one of the most beautiful experiences of my life. Come with me, and learn how to tell a travel story like a native speaker!

Chapter 1 · Day 1 · Rio de Janeiro

Copacabana: sand, sport and sunshine

I travelled with a friend on an organised tour with a travel agency, and the group was really well organised. We arrived in Rio de Janeiro after a thirteen-hour flight from Paris. On our first morning, we headed straight to Copacabana Beach, where we found loads of beach volleyball and beach soccer courts, as well as open-air gyms right on the sand.

Siamo arrivati a Rio de Janeiro dopo 13 ore di volo da Parigi, e la prima mattina siamo andati a Copacabana Beach, dove abbiamo trovato tanti campi da beach volley, beach soccer e palestre a cielo aperto.

Chapter 2 · Day 1 · Rio de Janeiro

Rocinha: two sides of the favela

Listen to chapter 2Click play to listen

In the afternoon, we visited Rocinha, one of Rio's favelas. We walked through narrow alleys full of people with guns in their pockets. Honestly, I felt more unsafe here than in Medellín. However, there are also social cooperatives that work hard to teach young people activities and sports like capoeira. The kids put on shows to raise funds, so that they don't get drawn into crime. That was our only sunny day in Rio.

🇮🇹 Dany’s original words

Nel pomeriggio abbiamo visitato la favela Rocinha, dove abbiamo camminato nei pericolosi vicoli pieni di persone con pistole nelle tasche. Qui ho percepito più pericolo, rispetto a Medellín. Chapter 3 · Days 2–3 · Rio de Janeiro

Hide-and-seek with Christ the Redeemer

The next day, we went to see Christ the Redeemer, but the rain and fog prevented us from seeing it properly. The statue was hidden in the fog! In the afternoon, we went up Sugarloaf Mountain, which let us see Rio from a different perspective. On the third day, we went back to Christ the Redeemer, hoping to see it this time. Luckily, the weather was better, and we finally got to see it. That evening, we flew to São Luís, in the state of Maranhão, in the north of Brazil.

Il terzo giorno siamo tornati sul Cristo Redentore con la speranza di vederlo, e per fortuna il tempo era migliore e lo abbiamo visto. La sera del terzo giorno siamo partiti in aereo per São Luís, a nord del Brasile, nello stato del Maranhão.

The white sands of the Lençóis Maranhenses

In Maranhão, we visited the Lençóis Maranhenses: a vast stretch of brilliant white sand with lots of freshwater lagoons where you can go for a swim. This landscape is unique in the world. From the Lençóis, we started to make our way towards Fortaleza. Along the way, we made several stops and used different means of transport: 4x4s, boats, and even small ferries that looked like rafts, but carried cars from one bank of the river to the other.

Chapter 6 · On the river

Thousands of scarlet ibises at sunset

During one boat transfer, we went on an excursion along the river to see the scarlet ibises: birds as big as seagulls, but completely red, because they eat red crabs. We saw them at sunset, just as they were getting ready to sleep on a small island in the middle of the river, where there are no predators. It was an incredible sight: thousands and thousands of ibises landing on the trees and turning them red. They looked like flowers!

🇮🇹 Dany’s original words

Chapter 7 · Ceará

Jericoacoara: sandy streets and a ride on Mars

In the north of Brazil, the towns were smaller and the streets were made of sand — not paved, not even gravel — so walking around was really hard work. This excursion was amazing, because I felt like I was driving on Mars: endless stretches of brilliant white sand, lit up by the sun and flattened by the wind.

04Culture corner: did you know…?

🌊

The famous wavy promenade

The black-and-white wave pattern you can see in Dany's picture is the Copacabana promenade (the calçadão). Its modern design is by the Brazilian landscape architect Roberto Burle Marx, in the 1970s. The wave idea comes from Portuguese pavements.

🏘️

Rocinha

Rocinha is one of the largest favelas in Brazil, built on a steep hillside between two of Rio's richest neighbourhoods. Many social projects there use sport, music and capoeira to offer young people an alternative to crime.

🥋

Capoeira

Capoeira is an Afro-Brazilian art that mixes martial arts, dance, acrobatics and music. It was created by enslaved Africans in Brazil and is now recognised by UNESCO as Intangible Cultural Heritage.

✝️

Christ the Redeemer

The statue on top of Corcovado mountain was completed in 1931 and is about 30 metres tall (plus its pedestal). In 2007 it was voted one of the New Seven Wonders of the World. Because of its height, it is often hidden in the clouds — just like on Dany's second day!

🚡

Sugarloaf Mountain

In Portuguese it's Pão de Açúcar. Its cable car opened in 1912, and was one of the first passenger cable cars in the world.

🏜️

Lençóis Maranhenses

It looks like a desert, but it isn't: the region gets a lot of rain. Rainwater collects between the dunes and forms the famous lagoons. It is a national park, and the lagoons are usually fullest in the middle of the year.

🦩

Why are scarlet ibises red?

Scarlet ibises (guarás in Portuguese) are red because of natural pigments called carotenoids in the crabs and shellfish they eat — the same reason flamingos are pink!

🍹

Caipirinha

Brazil's national cocktail is made with cachaça (a spirit made from sugar cane), lime and sugar. Today you can find it in all sorts of fruit flavours: passion fruit, strawberry, mango…

05Your storytelling toolkit

A great travel story has a shape. Here is Dany’s, in five steps — use it for your own trips.

1

Set the scene

Who, where, how, why.

  • I travelled with a friend on an organised tour.
  • We arrived in Rio after a thirteen-hour flight.
2

Move through time

Use time sequencers to guide your listener.

  • On our first morning, …
  • In the afternoon, … / That evening, …
  • The next day, … / On the third day, …
  • From there, … / Along the way, …
3

Share feelings & reactions

Your listener wants to feel the trip.

  • Honestly, I felt more unsafe here than…
  • It was an incredible sight!
  • I felt like I was driving on Mars.
4

Add a problem — and a twist

Every good story has a small drama.

  • Unfortunately, the fog prevented us from seeing it.
  • Luckily, the weather was better and we finally got to see it.
  • The road was terrible, but it was totally worth it!
5

Wrap it up

End with an evaluation or a recommendation.

  • All in all, it was one of the best trips of my life.
  • If you ever get the chance, you have to go!
  • I'd go back tomorrow.

Upgrade your adjectives (B2 boost)

Instead of very + adjective, use a stronger word. With strong adjectives, use absolutely or really, not very: absolutely stunning ✅ very stunning ❌

very beautiful → stunning / breathtaking / gorgeous
very big → huge / vast / endless
very good (food) → delicious / out of this world
very tired → exhausted
very interesting → fascinating
very bad (road) → terrible / awful
very surprised → amazed / astonished
very full (of people) → packed / crowded

Past simple or past continuous?

Past simple tells the main events, one after the other: We arrived in Rio… we headed to Copacabana… we visited Rocinha.

Past continuous paints the background — an action in progress: We saw them just as they were getting ready to sleep. I felt like I was driving on Mars.

06Italian traps: myth vs reality

These mistakes come from translating word for word from Italian. Click a card to see the natural English.

❌ We made an excursion on the river.
✅ We went on an excursion / took a trip along the river.

In English we go on or take an excursion, a trip, a tour — we don't 'make' it.

❌ We made the bath in the lagoons.
✅ We went for a swim in the lagoons.

'Fare il bagno' outdoors = go for a swim. 'Have a bath' only happens in a bathtub!

❌ We arrived to Rio.
✅ We arrived in Rio. / We arrived at the airport.

arrive in + city/country; arrive at + building/place. Never 'arrive to'.

❌ A thirteen-hours flight.
✅ A thirteen-hour flight.

In compound adjectives before a noun, the number + noun stays singular: a five-star hotel, a two-week holiday.

❌ The fog prevented us to see it.
✅ The fog prevented us from seeing it.

prevent someone from + -ing. Same with 'stop someone from doing'.

❌ The road shakered us.
✅ The road shook us up.

'Shakerare' comes from 'shaker', but the English verb is shake – shook – shaken.

❌ It valued the pain.
✅ It was worth it.

'Ne è valsa la pena' = it was worth it. A classic Italian translation trap!

❌ Lagoons of sweet water.
✅ Freshwater lagoons.

'Acqua dolce' = fresh water. 'Sweet water' sounds like water with sugar in it.

❌ There was a lot of movida.
✅ There was a lively nightlife / a great nightlife scene.

'Movida' is Spanish, and English speakers don't use it. Say nightlife.

❌ The streets were not asphalted.
✅ The streets were not paved.

'Asphalted' exists but sounds technical. The natural word is paved.

❌ We stopped three nights in Jericoacoara.
✅ We stayed (for) three nights in Jericoacoara.

'Fermarsi' in a place for nights = stay. 'Stop' is for a short break.

❌ The people were very sunny.
✅ The people were really warm and cheerful.

'Solare' about people is best translated as cheerful, upbeat or warm.

5 · Grammar in the story

  1. The rain and fog prevented us _____ it properly. → from seeing. prevent + object + from + -ing.
  2. We saw the ibises just as they _____ ready to sleep. → were getting. Past continuous for an action in progress at a moment in the past (the background of the story).
  3. We _____ in Rio after a thirteen-hour flight. → arrived. Past simple for completed actions in a finished time — the main events of a story.
  4. It was a _____ flight. → thirteen-hour. Compound adjective before a noun: the noun stays singular.
  5. Jericoacoara was quiet, but in high season it _____ really busy. → must be. must + base verb for a logical deduction (I'm almost sure).
  6. I felt like I _____ on Mars. → was driving. feel like + clause: the tense follows the story (past continuous = the experience in progress).
  7. You can only get there _____ 4x4. → by. Means of transport: by car / boat / plane / 4x4 (no article).
  8. The weather was better, so we finally _____ see it. → got to. get to + verb = have the chance to do something. 'Arrive to' and 'succeed to' are Italian-style errors.

6 · Everyday situations

  1. It was amazing, thanks — totally worth the long flight! — Natural, friendly, and uses 'worth'. 'We have made many excursions' has two errors: tense and 'make'.
  2. Are transfers between the airport and the hotel included? — Polite, clear question with 'included'. 'Inside the price' is a calque from Italian.
  3. Is it a long drive? I tend to get carsick. — 'Get carsick' = feel sick while travelling. 'I'm sick of cars' means you're tired of them!
  4. Unfortunately the fog meant we couldn't see anything. Is there any chance we could go back tomorrow? — Polite complaint + solution. Note: disappointed with/by, not 'of'.
  5. If you ever get the chance, you have to see the Lençóis — it's like nothing else on earth. — 'If you ever get the chance, you have to…' is the perfect recommendation formula.
  6. It's been lovely travelling with you all — let's keep in touch! — Warm and natural. 'Keep in touch' = stay in contact.

We started in Rio: on our first morning we headed straight to Copacabana, and in the afternoon we visited Rocinha, where I honestly felt quite unsafe. The next day, the fog prevented us from seeing Christ the Redeemer, but luckily we went back and finally got to see it. Then we flew north to Maranhão. The road to the Lençóis was really bumpy, but it was totally worth it: a vast stretch of white sand with freshwater lagoons. Along the way to Fortaleza we saw thousands of scarlet ibises at sunset — an incredible sight. And in Jericoacoara we rode quad bikes. I felt like I was driving on Mars! All in all, it was one of the best trips of my life.
